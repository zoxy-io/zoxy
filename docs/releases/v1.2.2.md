**zoxy 1.2.2: HTTP/3 slots come back when they should.** zoxy now
refuses a QUIC client it has no room for outright, instead of leaving it
to time out. And the pool stops filling with connections that are already
over, so far fewer clients need refusing: on the benchmark's c1k-h3
profile, none.

Replace the binary; no config changes.

## Fixes

- **A client the QUIC pool has no room for is refused, not left to time
  out** (#454). zoxy answers its first Initial with CONNECTION_CLOSE and
  CONNECTION_REFUSED (RFC 9000 §5.2.2), so the client gives up or falls
  back to TCP at once. Before, the Initial went unanswered and the client
  waited out its own handshake timeout. The refusal is a few dozen octets
  against the client's 1,200, so it amplifies nothing. Each one is counted
  in the new `zoxy_h3_refused`.

- **A closing connection gives its slot back at once** (#476). A
  connection in its closing or draining period (RFC 9000 §10.2, three
  PTOs) kept its whole slot until the period ended: the ~835 KiB of
  transport and stream buffers, and the TLS engine. Now the slot is free
  as soon as the connection's CONNECTION_CLOSE is out. A record of about
  300 B holds the rest of the period, which §10.2 asks a server not to
  cut short:
  - a late packet for the connection is not taken for a new connection
    or met with a stateless reset;
  - a closing connection's record answers it with the same close, at a
    falling rate and never more than three times what triggered it;
  - a draining connection's record answers nothing.

  The new `zoxy_h3_closes_repeated` counts those answers.

- **A retired connection whose client has gone closes within a fraction
  of a second, not a minute** (#475). Since 1.2.1, a connection that sent
  a GOAWAY waits for the client to acknowledge everything before closing.
  A client that leaves sends its last acknowledgement and its close once.
  When that datagram was lost, zoxy kept the connection, and its slot,
  for a full idle window. Now it stops waiting once three probe timeouts
  in a row go unanswered: seven PTOs of silence.

## Why these three ship together

#454 alone made things worse on the benchmark, which is why it waited
for the other two (zoxy-io/benchmark#21). c1k-h3 gives zoxy 1,024 QUIC
slots for 1,000 clients, and the pool overflowed twice in every run:

- at the start, where zrk gives up on handshakes that take it more than
  two seconds and opens new ones while the old ones are still closing;
- at `keepalive_requests`, where all 1,000 connections are retired and
  replaced within seconds.

Before #454 most of the clients turned away got in on a retransmitted
Initial a moment later. With #454 alone, each one became a failed request: 2,068
on the run that measured it, against 506 on 1.2.1. #475 and #476 free
the slots those connections held, and with all three nothing is refused.

## Under the hood

- **h3 moves from 004f04a to ba373f8** (zoxy-io/h3#13):
  `endpoint.writeRefusal`, the stateless Initial carrying the refusal.
  The audit is recorded beside the pin in `build.zig.zon`.
- **`zoxy --check` prices a QUIC connection about 300 B more** (its
  closing record), and the metrics render buffers 304 B larger (the two
  new counters).
- **The test client can vanish.** `TestClient.vanishes` goes silent
  once answered, the way a client whose last datagram was lost looks to
  the server.

## Checked against

- **The gates.** Every change passed the unit and fuzz corpus, the lint,
  4,096 simulator seeds with the census, and the live gate in both the
  default and the shipped ReleaseSafe build.
- **The tests.** Each new test fails with its fix removed:
  - a client the pool has no room for hears CONNECTION_REFUSED;
  - a client right behind a closing connection, with one QUIC slot, is
    served;
  - a late packet is answered with the close from its record, not a
    stateless reset;
  - a close the anti-amplification limit holds back keeps its slot
    until it goes out;
  - a retired connection whose client vanished frees its slot within
    2 s, where it held it for the idle window.
- **zoxy-io/benchmark's c1k-h3**: 1,000 HTTP/3 connections, 10 KiB
  answers, a ramp past saturation on one core, zrk 3.0.5, against nginx
  in the same run.

  | run | zoxy | refused | failed requests (zoxy / nginx) | sustained req/s (zoxy / nginx) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 45168255501 | 1.2.1 | — | 506 / 441 | 5,298 / 5,678 |
  | 45218693431 | #454 alone | 2,046 | 2,068 / 343 | 4,760 / 5,642 |
  | 45234088370 | this release | 0 | **1** / 14 | **5,785** / 6,340 |

  zoxy completed 1,655,378 requests to nginx's 1,765,861.
- **The soak.** A run on this release's code passed: 8 × 500,000
  seeds, 584,000,001 to 588,000,000, census ok on every shard,
  [run 45234680204](https://github.com/zoxy-io/zoxy/actions/runs/45234680204).

## Upgrading

Replace the binary; no config changes. A QUIC connection costs about
300 B more, priced in the banner. A client turned away for want of a
QUIC slot now hears CONNECTION_REFUSED rather than nothing, and a load
generator counts that as a failed connection rather than a slow one.
