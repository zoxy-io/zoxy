**zoxy 1.1: a build on the library takes part in the whole exchange.**
In 1.0, a build could pick endpoints and accept or refuse a request.
In 1.1 it can also:

- answer with pages of its own;
- edit headers both ways;
- tag a request across its phases;
- choose a request's route;
- refuse a connection before reading it;
- serve routes itself;
- change its endpoints while it serves;
- run health checks of its own;
- ask an outside service before deciding;
- hear how every pick and request ended;
- publish counters of its own.

The simulator drives nearly every one under its adversary, and
`examples/edge` holds most of them to the zero-allocation gate. For the
stock binary there is nothing to do: replace it. It lists no extensions,
so it behaves as 1.0.3 did. The new counters stay at zero; Upgrading
describes one narrow refusal and one renamed error.

## What a build can do

DESIGN §13 has every contract below in full.
[`examples/edge`](../../examples/edge) is a working build that shows
nearly all of them, with tests under the simulator and a live smoke. Its
README maps each hook to where it is shown.

### Deciding about a request

A request policy's verdict grows beyond proceed and refuse:

- **Answers of its own** (#431). A type declares named answers: a status
  from 300 to 599, headers and a body. `extension.respond(Self, .name)`
  answers with one, such as a `401` challenge, a maintenance `503` or a
  redirect to a login page. Each page is rendered once at startup and
  priced in the banner as `policy answers`. Headers that frame a
  response or name its sender stay zoxy's own, and declaring one is a
  compile error.
- **A tag** (#433). `proceed_with` may set the request's tag, a `u64`.
  Every later view carries it: the next policy, the route phase, and a
  picker on every try. The access log records a non-zero tag as `"tag"`.
- **Header edits** (#424). `set`, `add` and `remove`, the request
  filters' three, apply on the request's way upstream, in the order
  their deciders ran. They are copied into scratch every stream slot
  reserves, priced in the banner as `policy edits`. A name zoxy writes
  itself, or a value that is not a field value, gets a `400`.
- **Choosing the route** (#430). At the listener phase, a policy may name
  the route the request takes. Routes gain an optional code-only `name`,
  resolved once at `bind` with `PolicySite.routeNamed`.
- **The client certificate's names** (#432). On a mutual-TLS listener,
  the request view carries the verified certificate's URI and DNS names,
  such as a SPIFFE ID or a host, beside its fingerprint.
- **Asking a service first** (#437). The verdict `consult` names a
  plaintext HTTP/1.1 cluster and a request the type renders.
  - The request parks while zoxy runs the consult on the stream's own
    ops, over an upstream slot of the consulted cluster, under a timeout
    that narrows the request's deadline. No thread blocks.
  - `consulted` decides from the reply's status, head and bounded body.
  - A consult that cannot be made or answered takes the failure the type
    declares: `open` proceeds, `closed` answers `503`.
  - A request whose own deadline runs out during a consult is answered
    `504`.
  - Each consult holds one of `limits.consult_slots` buffers, set in
    code and priced in the banner as `consult slots`.
- **The clock and completions** (#429). The view carries the loop's
  `now_ns`. An optional `complete` hears, exactly once, how a request the
  policy let on ended, at the moment its access-log line is written: the
  count a rate or concurrency limit takes back down.

### New kinds of extension

- **Response policies** (#424), an ordered list per HTTP listener and per
  route. They read the origin's final response head and return header
  edits. They never see zoxy's own pages.
- **Connection policies** (#425), per listener of either protocol and per
  SNI route.
  - They run once the client's address is known: after the PROXY header,
    before any handshake, peek or head.
  - They proceed, tag the connection, or close it without a byte
    written: `reject` with a FIN, `reset` with an RST.
  - An SNI route's list also reads the name the hello asked for.
  - An optional `complete` hears each connection's release: the count a
    per-client connection cap takes down.
- **Handlers** (#434). A route may select a handler in place of a
  cluster, and the handler answers the route's requests itself from the
  head buffer: a status page, or the control plane a build serves. It
  reads the request's body whole: declared by its length on HTTP/1.1,
  to the stream's end on HTTP/2.
- **Health-check kinds** (#436). A cluster's check may be a build's own
  type. zoxy dials and holds the deadline. The type says what to send in
  up to `health_check_rounds_max` rounds and judges each reply: a
  database's greeting, a cache's `PING`. Plaintext only.

### Pickers and endpoints

- **Completions** (#423). An optional `complete` hears exactly once how
  each pick turned out. The outcome is one of the origin's whole response
  with its status, a failed dial, a timeout, an origin failure, a stale
  parked connection, a shed, an abort, or an L4 relay closed.
  - It carries the loop-clock times of the pick, of the connection, of
    the response head, and of the outcome: what a latency-steering picker
    folds in.
  - Retries and replays complete as picks of their own.
- **Live endpoints** (#428). A cluster may declare `endpoint_slots`, room
  for endpoints a build adds while it serves, priced at startup.
  - `Startup.endpointSet(name)` hands the build the cluster's set.
    `replace` swaps the whole set at once or not at all, on the loop
    thread: from a handler, a policy, or a picker's `complete`, never
    inside a pick or `endpointsChanged`.
  - An endpoint that stays keeps its slot, health, parked connections
    and sticky placements.
  - One that leaves drains; one that arrives takes a free slot, out on a
    checked cluster until its first probe passes.
  - Pickers may declare `endpointsChanged` to follow the change.

### A build's own metrics

**Its own series** (#435). A picker, request policy or connection policy
may declare `metrics_prefix`, `counters` and `gauges`.

- Each renders as `zoxy_ext_<prefix>_<name>`, after zoxy's own series,
  once per site that selects the type: `cluster`, `listener`, a route
  (`listener` and `route`, labelled by what it matches), or an SNI route
  (`sni`).
- Every serving call and completion carries its site's slots.
- `zoxy.testing.Bed.counter` reads them back in a build's tests.

### Testing a build

`zoxy.testing` exports a scripted double of every kind
(`ScriptedConsultPolicy`, `ScriptedConsultOpenPolicy`, `ScriptedHandler`,
`ScriptedProbe`, `ScriptedConnectionPolicy`, `ScriptedResponsePolicy`,
beside 1.0's two). zoxy's own simulator drives one of each kind under
the adversary, and every counter they declare is in its census.

## The stock binary

It runs no extensions, so its traffic, access log and Proxy-Status are
1.0.3's, and so is every config it loads but those under Upgrading.
What it reports changes:

- **The banner.**
  - It gains three lines that read `0 B` without extensions:
    `policy answers`, `policy edits` and `consult slots`.
  - Slots grow for the fields the new hooks keep per connection and per
    stream: conn slots 552 → 576 B, stream slots
    1840 → 1896 B. A TLS engine grows by the client
    certificate names it keeps.
  - The labeled-metrics figure rises too (34,674 → 39,662
    B on a one-listener, one-cluster config), mostly for the new counters'
    rows. It also includes a pricing fix: the endpoint and cluster tables
    had been priced short of what startup allocates.
- **Metrics.** 26 new counters, listed under Upgrading, each rendered at
  zero.
- **The JSON Schema.** It is 1.0.3's, except that
  `limits.access_log_buffer_bytes` has a minimum of 3863, up from 3836.
  No key was added: route names, `endpoint_slots` and `consult_slots`
  are code-only.

- **The gates.** Every change passed the unit and fuzz corpus, the lint,
  4,096 simulator seeds with the census, and the live gate in both the
  default and the shipped ReleaseSafe build.
- **The simulator.**
  - It drives every new hook under the adversary, over HTTP/1.1 and
    HTTP/2, except the client certificate's names, which no listener of
    the sweep asks for; unit tests cover those. Consults park HTTP/2
    streams side by side, and endpoint sets change while seeds serve.
  - Only the fail-open consulting double runs there; the fail-closed
    one is the unit tests'.
  - The census: 116 counters fired, 45 exempt; 23 extension counters
    fired; 9 seam operations, 1 exempt.
- **h2spec.** It matched DESIGN §12's recorded deviations: 145 cases
  over h2c with 1 recorded failure, and 145 over TLS with none.
- **The zero-allocation gate.** `examples/edge` holds its build to

## Upgrading

Replace the binary. Three things read differently:

- **`limits.access_log_buffer_bytes` from 3836 to 3862 is now refused**
  with `LimitAccessLogBufferOutOfRange`. The buffer must hold the longest
  line, and a line may now carry `"tag"`. The stock binary never writes
  a tag, but the bound is closed-form and does not know that. Raise the
  value to 3863 or more. `--check` names the error.
- **A route whose `cluster` is the empty string** is refused with
  `RouteTargetInvalid`, where 1.0.3 said `ClusterUnknown`. It was refused
  before, too.
- **A listener with more than 65,535 routes** is refused with
  `RoutesOverLimit`. A policy names a route by a 16-bit index.

A build on the library moves with the seam, which is published but not
frozen. Code written against 1.0.3 needs these changes:

- **Struct literals of views** gain fields with no default:
  `extension.Request.tag` and `.now_ns`, `extension.PickInput.tag`, and
  `extension.PolicySite.clusters` and `.routes`. Tests that build a view
  by hand set them.
- **Unions and enums with new arms**: `extension.Verdict`
  (`proceed_with`, `respond`, `consult`), `Options.Cluster.Check.Kind`
  and its tag, the `config.Config.Cluster.Check.Kind` enum (`extension`).
  An exhaustive `switch` over any needs the new arms.
  `Config.Cluster.Check.Builtin` names the three kinds a JSON config
  may.
- **Declarations that now mean something.**
  - A picker's `pub` `complete` and `endpointsChanged` are read as the
    hooks of those names.
  - A request policy's `pub` `complete`, `answers`, `consulted` and
    `edit_bytes_max` are too. A type that declares `consulted` must also
    declare `consult_failure` and the three consult bounds.
  - On either, `metrics_prefix`, `counters` and `gauges` declare its
    series.

  One meant otherwise is now a compile error.
- **On a handler route**, a policy's `PolicySite` has a `route_index`
  and a null `cluster_index`, and the request view's `cluster_index`
  stays null. A null cluster no longer means the listener's own site,
  and a route no longer implies a cluster.
- **`App.serve`'s error set** gains the new startup refusals:
  - `AnswerHeadOverBuffer`
  - `HandlerResponseOverBuffer`
  - `PolicyEditsOverLimit`
  - `ConsultSlotsRequired`
  - `RouteNameInvalid`
  - `RouteNameDuplicate`
  - `RoutesOverLimit`
  - `RouteTargetInvalid`
  - `EndpointSlotsOutOfRange`
  - `LimitConsultSlotsOverUpstreamSlots`
  - `ClusterTlsExtensionCheck`

New counters, every one at zero in the stock binary:

- `conn_policy_rejected`, `conn_policy_reset` (#425)
- `l7_policy_routed`, `l7_policy_route_fallback` (#430)
- `l7_policy_edit_refused`, `l7_response_policy_edit_refused` (#424)
- `l7_policy_consult_started`, `_replied`, `_failed`, `_refused`,
  `_oversize`, `_slots_exhausted`, `_shed`, `_timed_out`, `_expired`
  (#437)
- `l7_handled`, `l7_handler_body_too_large`, `l7_handler_answer_refused`
  (#434)
- `health_probe_rounds_exhausted`, `health_probe_request_oversize` (#436)
- `health_endpoint_down_arrival`, `endpoints_arrived`,
  `endpoints_departed`, `endpoints_reclaimed`, `endpoints_reweighted`,
  `endpoints_parked_closed` (#428)
